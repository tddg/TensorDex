1 · The problem: misses have state-dependent costs

The hot cache stores reconstructed tensors. A request for tensor x (logical size L, delta size 0.34 L on average) that misses is served by fetching from origin and decoding at the cache. What that miss costs depends on what else is resident:

cache state at miss timeorigin bytescache CPUnote
x’s base resident (pinned, or hot as a servable tensor)0.34 L1 decodecheap miss
base not resident1.34 L1 decodeexpensive miss — and whether the base stays decides the cost of every sibling’s future miss
x is raw (base / unattached)1.0 L0classic miss

Hit-rate-maximizing policies (LRU, LFU, size-aware variants) optimize hit fraction. But the tier’s throughput and latency are governed by origin bytes moved + decodes performed per unit time. Those are different objectives, and the gap between them grows with the DAG’s fan-in skew. A resident base is never “hit” by most requests — it silently converts expensive misses into cheap ones for thousands of descendants.

Bases sorted by descendant count, x = cumulative base capacity. The knee is extreme: 29 GB of base capacity discounts misses for half of all 581k deltas; 162 GB covers 90%; the full base set is 405 GB — about 6% of one i4i.8xlarge’s NVMe. Fan-in: p50 25, p99 589, max 7,459.

3 · The policy spectrum to evaluate

LRU / size-aware LRUhit-rate anchor; DAG-blind. Expected to waste capacity on giant reconstructions whose bases then get evicted.
Base-pinned + LRU remainderlower-bound anchor: pin all 405 GB of bases; every delta request misses cheap. Maximum miss rate, minimum per-miss cost.
GDSF (cost-aware)classic miss-cost caching with static per-item cost. The strongest prior-art baseline — but costs here aren’t static.
dag_value (ours)value(x) = own traffic + Σ resident-descendant miss discount, recomputed as residency changes. Greedy knapsack over coupled utilities.

What’s new vs prior art: cost-aware caching (GreedyDual-Size family) assumes each item has a fixed miss cost. Here the miss cost of x is a function of the cache’s current contents (base resident or not), utilities couple through the DAG (non-modular value), and items are dual-role — every base is also a servable model tensor, so its value is own-traffic + descendant-discount. Offline oracle (ILP/greedy on the trace) bounds the achievable gap.

4 · Hypotheses

  1. H1: at small-to-mid capacity (cache ≪ working set), DAG-aware allocation beats LRU on origin traffic by a large factor (the 29 GB → 50% knee makes the head bases nearly free to keep); the policies converge as capacity approaches the working set.
  2. H2: hit rate and byte-weighted miss cost rank policies differently — the experiment that shows a policy with a worse hit rate delivering better e2e latency is the paper’s key figure.
  3. H3: at high concurrency, the decode-CPU term of cheap misses becomes visible, merging the eviction question with the store-format question (cache deltas vs reconstructions) into a single capacity-vs-CPU frontier.
  4. H4: ancestor pre-warming (serving model A warms bases shared with model B) yields measurable cross-model latency wins under popularity-correlated workloads — the effect already observed accidentally in the i4i campaign (§4).
